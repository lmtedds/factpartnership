Compliance by Design: Why Businesses Fail to Report Income, and Why the Answer Lies in Tax Administration as State Infrastructure

Lindsay M. Tedds

Opening remarks and written brief to the Senate Banking Committee for its study of Canada’s underground economy. Shows that whether income is reported depends mainly on whether a third party also reports it, and that tax administration, compliance and benefit delivery run on the same information. Recommends building ePayroll and platform reporting as shared infrastructure, extending point-of-sale visibility on Quebec’s model, restoring random audits with researcher access to CRA compliance data, and enforcing existing reporting through software that records information once and reports it to every agency that needs it.

Allocution d’ouverture et mémoire présentés au Comité sénatorial permanent des banques, du commerce et de l’économie dans le cadre de son étude sur l’économie souterraine au Canada. Le document montre que la déclaration des revenus dépend surtout de la présence d’un tiers qui les déclare aussi, et que l’administration fiscale, l’observation et le versement des prestations reposent sur les mêmes informations. Il recommande de concevoir la paie électronique et la déclaration par les plateformes numériques comme une infrastructure commune, d’étendre la visibilité des ventes au point de vente selon le modèle québécois, de rétablir les vérifications aléatoires en donnant aux chercheurs accès aux données d’observation de l’ARC, et de faire respecter les obligations de déclaration existantes au moyen de logiciels qui saisissent l’information une seule fois et la transmettent à tous les organismes qui en ont besoin.

27 October 2026 Parliamentary testimony

Tedds to appear before the Senate Banking Committee on Canada’s underground economy

FACT Project Director Dr. Lindsay M. Tedds appears before the Standing Senate Committee on Banking, Commerce and the Economy on October 28, 2026, as part of the committee’s study of Canada’s underground economy. Her opening remarks and written brief are available below.

The committee asked why businesses fail to report income. The brief’s answer is that reporting depends mainly on visibility. In the United Kingdom, which measures compliance each year through random audits, the tax gap on wages reported through real-time payroll is about 1 per cent, while the gap on self-employment income is about 29 per cent. Canada’s income tax still rests on a self-assessment model that has changed little since 1917, and the Canada Revenue Agency’s estimate of small-business compliance rests on a random audit of tax year 2011.

Because the CRA also delivers most income-tested federal benefits, the same information gaps that let income go unreported also cause benefits to be missed or mistimed. The brief draws on FACT’s work on tax administration as state infrastructure and recommends building ePayroll and the digital platform reporting rules as shared infrastructure for compliance and benefits, extending point-of-sale visibility on Quebec’s model, restoring a published random audit program with researcher access to CRA compliance data, and enforcing existing reporting requirements through software that records information once and reports it to every agency that needs it.

Compliance is not a matter of character. It is something we can design for.

— Dr. Lindsay M. Tedds, opening remarks to the Standing Senate Committee on Banking, Commerce and the Economy
